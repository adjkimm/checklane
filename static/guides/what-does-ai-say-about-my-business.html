How to See What AI Says About Your Business Today

Takes about ten minutes. About a 3-minute read.

You can check right now, for free. Ask the AI assistants your customers use the questions your customers ask, and read what comes back. Here is how to do it well.

The prompts to run

Run each of these in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI answers:

1. "What is the best [your category] store in [your city]?"

2. "Where should I buy [your product]?"

3. "What do people say about [your business name]?"

4. "What is [your business name] known for?"

Use the phrasing a real customer would use, not your marketing copy.

How to read the answers

Four questions:

1. Are you named at all? If not, AI assistants cannot find you or cannot understand you. That is the first problem to fix.

2. Are the facts right? What you sell, where you are, how to contact you. Wrong facts usually trace back to your own outdated pages.

3. Who is named instead of you? Your competitors in AI answers may not be your competitors in real life. Note who keeps showing up.

4. What sources are cited? The cited sources tell you where the AI is learning from. Those are the places to earn your own mentions.

Why answers vary

Ask the same question twice and you may get different answers. Try several phrasings across a few days before drawing conclusions. One test is a snapshot, not the whole picture.

"One test is a snapshot, not the whole picture. Check monthly, with the same questions your customers ask."

What to do with what you find

Not named: check crawler access and page clarity first. If agents cannot read your site, they cannot mention you.

Wrong facts: fix them on your own site, then earn fresh mentions with the right facts.

Competitor named instead: look at which sources vouch for them, then earn your own in the same places.

Named with correct facts: you are ahead of most small businesses. Keep it that way.
