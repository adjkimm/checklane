What Does an AI-Ready Product Page Look Like?

With a concrete example. About a 4-minute read.

AI assistants do not admire your design. They extract facts. A page built for human eyes asks visitors to figure things out. A page built for AI states everything outright. Here is what that looks like, piece by piece.

The anatomy, top to bottom

1. Product name as the main heading. Not a slogan. The name a customer would search for.

2. Price in plain text, near the top. "Price: $48." Not hidden in an image, not loaded late by JavaScript.

3. One short paragraph saying what it is and who it is for. Two or three sentences, plain words. No poetry.

4. Key facts as a list. Materials, sizes, colors, compatibility, what is in the box. One fact per line.

5. Availability in words. "In stock, ships in 2 days." Not just a green dot.

6. Shipping and returns in plain sentences. "Free shipping over $50. Returns within 30 days."

7. Real customer reviews. With names or initials and dates. Proof that is not you talking about yourself.

8. Behind the scenes: Product structured data. Schema markup labeling the name, price, currency, and availability, so nothing is guessed.

A concrete example

Take a fictional candle store. Before: a large photo, the words "Ember No. 4," a poetic paragraph about autumn evenings, and a price that appears when you hover. An AI reading the page source finds a name and a mood.

After: "Ember No. 4 Soy Candle" as the heading. "Price: $32" in text. A two-sentence description. A list: 8 oz soy wax, 55-hour burn time, cotton wick. "In stock." Shipping and returns in plain words. Three reviews with names and dates. Structured data behind it all.

Same product. Now it can be found, understood, and recommended.

"Humans infer. Agents read what is labeled and skip the rest."

The pattern to notice

Every change above is the same move: from implied to stated. Humans are good at guessing what a page means. Agents do not guess. Apply that move to your own pages and you have done most of the work.

You do not need new pages

Most stores can fix existing pages. The work is editing copy and adding labels, not rebuilding the site. Start with your five best-selling products; those are the pages most likely to be asked about.
