How to Check If Your Website Is Ready for AI Shopping Agents

No technical background required. About a 5-minute read.

AI agents now shop for your human shoppers. Online buying increasingly starts with an agent reading product pages, comparing options, and carrying out checkout steps on a person's behalf. If an agent cannot read your site, you lose the sale before a human shopper ever sees it. This guide walks you through exactly how to check your own site, step by step.

What does "ready for AI shopping agents" mean?

A website is ready for AI shopping agents when an agent can find the business, read its product pages, and follow the steps needed to check out, using only the public pages of the site.

Readiness is a practical question, not a mysterious one. Think of an agent as a very fast reader with no eyes: it does not see your design, your photos, or your layout. It reads text, links, prices, and buttons. Every part of your buying process that depends on seeing the page is a part the agent may fail.

"If an agent cannot read it, the agent cannot buy from it."

The three checks below cover the full path: finding you, reading your products, and completing a purchase.

Can AI agents find your business?

Agents find businesses through public signals: a clear business name, contact details, and pages that load without logins, pop-up walls, or crawler blocks.

Start with the basics. Your business name, phone number, and address should appear as plain text on your site, ideally in the footer of every page or on a contact page. Your product pages must load for a first-time visitor with no account and no login. Check your robots.txt file (yoursite.com/robots.txt) and make sure it does not block product or category pages; a single misplaced rule can hide your whole catalog from every automated reader. A sitemap (yoursite.com/sitemap.xml) helps agents discover pages the same way it helps search engines.

"An agent that cannot reach your product page will move on to a site it can reach."

Can agents read your product pages?

Agents read text, prices, options, and availability. If key details live only inside images, or appear only when a visitor hovers or clicks, agents will miss them.

Open one of your product pages and ask: is the price written as text, or is it baked into a product image? Are sizes, colors, and variants listed as text the agent can read, or only visible in a photo? Is stock status ("in stock," "ships in 2 days") stated in words? Product descriptions should be real paragraphs, not text flattened into an image file. Every detail a human shopper needs in order to decide is a detail the agent needs in readable form.

"What the agent cannot read, the agent cannot compare, and what it cannot compare, it cannot buy."

Can an agent follow your checkout steps?

A ready site lets an agent move from product page to cart to checkout without dead ends, broken forms, or steps that only work with a mouse and eyes.

Walk the path yourself, slowly, and note every step: add to cart, view cart, enter shipping, enter payment, confirm. Each step needs clearly labeled buttons ("Add to cart," "Checkout," "Place order") as real text, not images. Forms need visible labels for every field so an agent can tell the email box from the address box. Guest checkout matters: forcing account creation adds a step many agents cannot complete. Test on a phone too, so you catch layout issues that hide buttons or labels.

"Every extra step between the product page and the order button is a place where an agent can get lost."

What should you do after you get your score?

Fix the highest-impact issues first, starting with anything that blocks agents from finding or reading your pages.

Work in this order. First, unblock discovery: fix robots.txt rules, login walls, and missing contact details. Second, make product pages readable: prices, variants, and stock as text. Third, smooth the checkout path: labels, guest checkout, clear buttons. Then re-run the audit and confirm the score moved. Re-check quarterly, or whenever you redesign pages, add products, or change your checkout flow.

"Fix what blocks the agent before you polish what pleases the human."

Small structural fixes usually move the score more than any redesign.
