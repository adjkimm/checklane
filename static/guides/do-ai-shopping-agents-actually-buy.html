Do AI Shopping Agents Actually Buy Things, or Just Browse?

An honest look at where agentic commerce stands. About a 3-minute read.

Short answer: mostly they research. Longer answer: that still decides where the money goes. Here is where things actually stand, without the hype.

What agents do well today

Research and comparison. An agent can read ten product pages, compare prices and specs, and summarize the options in seconds. This is the part that already works reliably, and it is the part that decides which store gets the sale.

Where buying gets hard

Checkout is the hard part: payment credentials, trust, returns, edge cases. Most shopping agents today hand the final purchase back to the human. Fully agent-completed checkout exists in early forms, but it is not the normal way people buy yet.

Why the recommendation is the sale

Here is what matters for your store: even when a human clicks the final button, the agent chose the shortlist. If the agent never mentions you, you never get the chance. Being recommended is the new ranking.

"Even when a human clicks the final button, the agent chose the shortlist. Being recommended is the new ranking."

What this means for a store owner

You do not need to rebuild checkout for agents. You need to be findable, readable, and trusted by the systems doing the recommending. That work pays off today, in the research phase, and it is the same work that prepares you for the day agents complete purchases themselves.

Is this just hype?

The checkout part is early. The shift underneath is not: shoppers are asking AI instead of scrolling link lists, and that behavior is already here. The stores winning those answers are the ones whose sites machines can read.
