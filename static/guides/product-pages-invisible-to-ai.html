Why Your Product Pages Are Invisible to AI (And How to Fix Them)

No technical background required. About a 5-minute read.

Your product page looks perfect to you. The photos are beautiful, the layout is clean, the price is right there. But an AI shopping agent visiting that same page may see almost nothing: no price, no options, no way to buy. The page is invisible to AI, and you would never know from looking at it yourself. Here are the five traps that cause it, and the fix for each.

Trap 1: The price lives inside an image

Some sites display prices as part of a designed graphic: a sale banner, a styled price tag baked into the product photo. A human sees "$49.99" clearly. An agent reading the page's text sees nothing where the price should be, because there is no price in the text at all.

The fix: make sure the price appears as real text on the page, next to or below any graphic. Sale prices should show both the original and sale price as text.

Trap 2: Options only exist in photos

"Available in red, blue, and green," says the product photo showing three color swatches. But the page text never lists the colors, and the variant selector is an unlabeled set of clickable dots. A human figures it out instantly. An agent cannot tell what the options are, which means it cannot match the shopper's request for "the blue one."

The fix: list every variant (size, color, style) as text on the page, and make sure selectors have text labels a machine can read.

"If the agent cannot name the option, the agent cannot choose it."

Trap 3: Stock status shown only by color or icon

A green dot means in stock. A grayed-out button means sold out. A human reads these cues without thinking. An agent sees a dot with no label and a button with no explanation. It cannot tell whether the product is available, and an agent will not gamble a shopper's money on a guess.

The fix: state availability in words next to the visual cue: "In stock," "Only 3 left," "Ships in 2 days." Keep the pretty dot. Just add the words.

Trap 4: The description is a picture of words

Long, beautifully typeset product descriptions are sometimes uploaded as images to preserve the design. Every word in that image is invisible to an agent. As far as any machine reader is concerned, the product has no description at all.

The fix: publish the description as real page text. You can keep styled images lower on the page for humans, but the words themselves must exist as text.

"Text flattened into an image is decoration, not information."

Trap 5: Key details hide behind hovers and clicks

Shipping info appears when you hover. Size charts open in a pop-up. The "details" tab needs a click. Humans do this naturally. Agents often cannot trigger hover states, and may not think to open every tab. Anything the agent never opens might as well not exist.

The fix: put the essentials (price, options, availability, shipping basics) directly on the page where they load. Nice-to-have details can stay behind tabs, but nothing required for a buying decision should.

How to check your own pages in ten minutes

Open your best-selling product page and try this: select the price with your cursor. If you cannot highlight it as text, an agent cannot read it. Do the same for the options, the stock status, and the first paragraph of the description. Then view the page with images turned off (most browsers can do this in settings): whatever is left is roughly what an agent sees. What remains should still let a stranger understand and buy the product.

"Browse your own page with the images off. What is left is what the agent gets."
