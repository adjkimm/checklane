Can AI Agents Access My Website?

No technical background required. About a 3-minute read.

Yes. AI agents can access almost any public website. They open your pages the same way a browser does, read the text, and follow links. The real question is not whether they can reach your site. It is whether anything is stopping them, and whether they can understand what they find once they are in.

How agents get in

An AI shopping agent visits your site like a very fast reader. It requests the page, reads the HTML underneath, and pulls out what matters: product names, prices, availability, shipping terms. Some agents run full browsers and can click through pages and fill in forms. Others only read the raw page source and never run your JavaScript.

That split matters. If your prices and product details only appear after JavaScript runs, the simpler agents see an empty page. Your site is technically reachable and practically invisible.

What can block them

Four common blockers keep agents out:

1. robots.txt rules. Your robots.txt file can name AI crawlers like GPTBot or ClaudeBot and tell them to stay out. Many sites added these blocks on reflex. Respectful crawlers obey them.

2. Bot protection. Security tools sometimes treat AI visitors like attackers. A challenge page or a block aimed at scrapers can catch legitimate shopping agents too.

3. Login walls. Anything behind an account is invisible to an agent shopping on a stranger's behalf.

4. CAPTCHAs. A human test on key pages stops agents cold. They cannot solve it and they will not ask the shopper to.

"Every agent you block is an AI assistant that will recommend a competitor it can read instead."

How to check your own site

Three checks, under a minute total:

1. Read your robots.txt. Open yoursite.com/robots.txt in a browser. Look for the names GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, or Google-Extended next to the word Disallow. Each one is an AI system you are turning away.

2. Turn off JavaScript. Load your product pages with JavaScript disabled. If the price, description, or buy button disappears, agents that do not run JavaScript cannot see them either.

3. Run the free audit. Our audit tests your live site against 62 checks: crawler access, JavaScript dependence, structured data, and everything else an agent needs. It takes about ten seconds.

Getting in is only half of it

Access is step one. Step two is understanding. An agent that can open your product page but cannot find the price will move on to a store where it can. Clear headings, plain-text prices, and structured data that labels each fact are what turn a readable page into a shoppable one.

This is the gap most small stores have. Their sites were built for human eyes, and humans are good at guessing. Agents do not guess. They read what is labeled and skip the rest.
